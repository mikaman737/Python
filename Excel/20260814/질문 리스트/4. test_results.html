3. Why I believe this finding

"Significant in one subgroup, not the other" is the single most common way a false positive gets published. It survived four checks.

  • 1
    Formal interaction test. Comparing two p-values across split samples is invalid — the correct test asks whether the gender effect differs by lodging. Two-way ANOVA: satisfaction F = 6.87, p = 0.0088; recommendation F = 6.66, p = 0.0099. Revisit intention is marginal at p = 0.0529.
  • 2
    Multiple-comparison correction. Survives Benjamini–Hochberg FDR applied across all 18 tests, not just within its own family — the strictest version. Adjusted p = 0.0095 / 0.0118 / 0.0248.
  • 3
    Non-parametric agreement. Mann–Whitney U reaches the same verdict on all three (p = 0.029 / 0.033 / 0.028), so it isn't an artefact of the normality assumption.
  • 4
    Covariate adjustment. Controlling for age, household income, education, companions, lodging payment and lodging type, the gender coefficient barely moves: +0.093, +0.099, +0.099, all still p < 0.05. Men and women in this subgroup were also statistically comparable on every covariate tested (all p > 0.15), so it isn't a composition effect.
But hold the effect size in view. Cohen's d is 0.17–0.19 — "small" by convention. In the adjusted models, gender explains under 0.5% of the variance (adjusted R² ≈ 0.004–0.005), and the interaction term accounts for about 0.2%. This is a real but very small difference. It is honest to report it as statistically detectable and practically minor; it would be misleading to describe lodging travellers as showing a meaningful gender divide in satisfaction.

These nulls are informative rather than inconclusive. At n = 2,878 the study can detect d ≈ 0.10 with about 80% power, and the observed effects sit well below that. For marital status the largest gap across all nine tests was 0.05 points on a 5-point scale — small enough to treat as no relationship, not as a failure to find one.

Note the cancellation. Gender is significant among lodging travellers and null overall. The two subgroups push in opposite directions and average each other out. Had you tested only the pooled sample, you would have concluded "no gender effect" and missed it entirely — an instance of Simpson's-paradox-style masking.

5. Limitations

  • This is a subgroup finding, and it was not pre-registered. It emerged from splitting the data. The interaction test and FDR correction support it, but the honest status is a hypothesis worth confirming on new data, not a settled result.
  • Ceiling effect. Mean 4.30 of 5, with 40.6% of travellers at 4.5 or above. The compressed upper range attenuates any difference the instrument could show.
  • "With lodging" is defined by a payment record. 31 trips have a lodging visit-area but no payment, and are classified as no-lodging. A different definition could shift the subgroup boundary and therefore the result.
  • Aggregation. Each row is a per-traveller mean across visit areas, so within-trip variation is averaged away before testing.
An alternative reading. The result is framed as "men who stay overnight are more satisfied," but lodging is not randomly assigned. Travellers who book paid accommodation differ from day-trippers in trip length, distance and budget, and those differences may not be identical for men and women in ways the six covariates here do not capture. The same data equally supports "a certain kind of trip that men take more often produces higher ratings" — the design cannot separate the two.
Method notes
  • Welch's t-test throughout (does not assume equal variances); Levene's test was non-significant in every comparison, so Student's t would give near-identical results.
  • Cohen's d computed with the pooled standard deviation.
  • Marital status restricted to single (code 1) vs married (code 2); widowed, divorced and other (47 cases, 1.6%) excluded as too small to test.
  • Outcomes are per-traveller means over visit areas with VISIT_AREA_TYPE_CD 1–13; home, friend's home, office and lodging excluded.
  • Two travellers dropped — no qualifying visit areas, so no score could be computed.
  • Interaction tested via Type II ANOVA on outcome ~ gender × lodging.
