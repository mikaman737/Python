Mean far exceeds median — the classic signature of a right-skewed spend variable. For any test involving this variable, use the median and a rank-based method, or log-transform first.

45 trips have a lodging payment of ₩0. These are real rows in the source data, not import errors. They could be free or comped stays, points redemptions, or missing amounts recorded as zero — the data cannot distinguish these. Decide explicitly whether to keep, drop, or treat them as missing before running any spend analysis.

3. Notes for analysis

  • Two structurally missing columns. PAYMENT_AMT_WON and LODGING_TYPE_CD are missing for all 2,127 no-lodging travellers by definition, not by data loss. Any analysis using them is confined to n = 753.
  • Two genuinely missing rows. The three rating measures are missing for 2 travellers who had no visit areas of type 1–13, so nothing could be averaged.
  • Three variables are ordinal codes, not quantities. EDU_NM (1–8), HOUSE_INCOME (1–12) and AGE_GRP (10-year bands). Means are reported for completeness but the spacing between codes is not guaranteed equal — prefer Spearman or ordinal methods over Pearson correlation.
  • Sparse categories are widespread. Counting cells under n = 40: marital status 3 of 5, education 3 of 8, lodging type 7 of 11, residence 10 of 17. All need collapsing before testing, and the collapsing rule belongs in the write-up.
  • Two heavily skewed continuous variables. Lodging payment (skew 2.52) and companions (skew 3.76, kurtosis 30.6) both violate normality substantially — unlike the rating measures, which are only mildly skewed.
An alternative reading of the ₩0 payments. Treating those 45 rows as valid spend pulls the mean down and inflates the left tail; treating them as missing raises the mean and narrows n to 708. Neither is obviously right, and the choice moves every payment statistic on this page. The data records an amount, not whether that amount means "free" or "unknown."
